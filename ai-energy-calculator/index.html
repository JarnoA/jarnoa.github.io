CSY Lab · AI Energy Calculator

Your device or AI?

Compare the electricity and water of doing something yourself with doing it with AI. Then look at the same question three other ways.

For fun and for thinking. Not a measurement.

This calculator is entertainment and gives only a rough direction. Every number is an estimate, and the range between low and high can be tenfold or more. AI companies publish very little. The calculator does not tell you what to do. It shows alternative ways to look at the same question.

What this calculator leaves out

It is not the whole picture

Leaving these out is a choice. Here is why, so you can decide whether it matters for you.

Training the AI

Before anyone uses a model, it is trained. The water linked to training GPT-4 was about 600 million litres, 237 Olympic pools (UNU-INWEH 2026). Shared across all users, it is hard to divide per prompt, so it is not included.

Making the machines

Chips, servers and phones are made and thrown away. AI's e-waste could reach 2.5 million tonnes a year by 2030, nearly 250 Eiffel Towers (UNU-INWEH 2026). Not included.

Doing more because it is easy

When something becomes cheaper and faster, we often do more of it. An AI image may not replace two hours of work; it may come on top of it. This is called the rebound effect (Luccioni, Strubell & Crawford, FAccT 2025). The calculator assumes one replaces the other. That is generous to AI.

Your own life

Some studies count a person's whole carbon footprint as the cost of "doing it yourself", and AI then looks 130–1,500 times better (Tomlinson et al., Scientific Reports 2024). We do not: you eat and live whether you use AI or not.

Networks and waiting

Sending data and your device waiting while the AI works use a little energy. For one person this is small, so it is not included.

Which model, where, when

The same question can use ten times more energy on a bigger model, and the water depends on the climate and the power plant. Companies rarely say which. That is why every result here is a range.

Sources

Where the numbers come from

Most numbers about AI's energy are not from peer-reviewed research yet. Each source is labelled so you can see what kind it is.

  1. Peer-reviewed Oviedo, F., Kazhamiaka, F., Choukse, E. et al. (2025). Energy use of AI inference: efficiency pathways and test-time compute. Joule. Text: median 0.31 Wh per query (middle half 0.16–0.60 Wh); about 4.3 Wh with 15× more tokens. arxiv.org/abs/2509.20241
  2. Peer-reviewed Luccioni, A. S., Jernite, Y. & Strubell, E. (2024). Power Hungry Processing: Watts Driving the Cost of AI Deployment? ACM FAccT 2024. Image: average 2.9 Wh (as used by UNU-INWEH 2026). arxiv.org/abs/2311.16863
  3. Peer-reviewed Li, P., Yang, J., Islam, M. A. & Ren, S. Making AI Less "Thirsty". Communications of the ACM. Water taken vs water consumed; on-site and power-plant water. doi.org/10.1145/3724499
  4. Peer-reviewed Luccioni, A. S., Strubell, E. & Crawford, K. (2025). From Efficiency Gains to Rebound Effects: The Problem of Jevons' Paradox in AI's Polarized Environmental Debate. ACM FAccT 2025. arxiv.org/abs/2501.16548
  5. Peer-reviewed Tomlinson, B., Black, R. W., Patterson, D. J. & Torrance, A. W. (2024). The carbon emissions of writing and illustrating are lower for AI than for humans. Scientific Reports. nature.com/articles/s41598-024-54271-x
  6. Report UN University (UNU-INWEH) (2026). Environmental Cost of AI's Energy Use: Carbon, Water and Land Footprints. Image 2.9 Wh / 28.6 ml; complex video 415 Wh / 4.1 L; ChatGPT 383 GWh a year; AI 93 TWh in 2025; ~20 % of data-centre electricity; 130 kWh home electricity per person a year in sub-Saharan Africa. doi.org/10.53328/INR26RMA002 · Press release 3.6.2026 ("please" and "thank you"; 87–98 GWh ≈ nearly 760,000 people): eurekalert.org
  7. Report Delavande, J., Pierrard, R. & Luccioni, S. (2025). Video Killed the Energy Budget: Characterizing the Latency and Power Regimes of Open Text-to-Video Models. NeurIPS 2025 workshop. Open video models from about 0.14 Wh to over 100 Wh per clip. arxiv.org/abs/2509.19222
  8. Company Google (2025). Measuring the environmental impact of AI inference. Median Gemini text prompt 0.24 Wh and 0.26 ml of on-site water. via MIT Technology Review
  9. News O'Donnell, J. & Crownhart, C. (2025). We did the math on AI's energy footprint. MIT Technology Review, 20.5.2025. Image (Stable Diffusion 3 Medium) about 0.6–1.2 Wh; 5-second video (CogVideoX) about 940 Wh. technologyreview.com
  10. News Taylor, L. (2026). Ditch the niceties in AI prompts to save energy use, say researchers. New Scientist, 3.6.2026. newscientist.com
  11. News Kaleva 15.10.2025 (Muhos river water) · Yle 8.10.2026 (Muhos and Kajaani forest) · DW and Mongabay (Montevideo 2023).
  12. Estimate Device watts. Phones, laptops and desktops are measured by testers and researchers, but there is no single peer-reviewed number for "a laptop in everyday work". We use ranges. Phone power is mostly the screen and the radio (Carroll & Heiser, USENIX 2010).
